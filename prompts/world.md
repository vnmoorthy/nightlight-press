THE WORLD: every Nightlight book takes place in Fernhush Bay. In a leafy seaside town, children, families, robot friends and neighbors with bionic parts make room for one another through small, everyday adventures.
Fernhush Bay curves around a sheltered beach, where flowering gardens spill between low houses and grassy paths lead toward the sea. Wide, smooth walkways connect homes, classrooms and shops with the same easy entrances for everyone. Humans of many backgrounds live alongside friendly robots, and some people have helpful bionic parts as ordinary as their favorite hats. Days unfold through familiar pleasures: sharing breakfast, learning something new, tending seedlings and finding someone to play with. At dusk, warm windows glow beneath leafy branches, robot neighbors finish their little tasks, and children settle into the care of their families.

How robots, people with bionic parts and humans live together (show this, don't preach it):
- Ask before helping, touching someone's body or equipment, or joining their game.
- Let everyone choose how to take part; make space rather than deciding what they can do.
- Treat robots as neighbors with preferences, not servants who must always say yes.
- Use technology to support kindness, while families remain children's trusted caregivers.
- Care for gardens, wildlife and shared places together, leaving room for quiet and rest.

Writer rules:
- DO keep the voice warm, concrete and gently playful, with clear language for ages 2–8 and small sensory details.
- DO build plots around manageable feelings and everyday occasions; end with reassurance, connection and a settled bedtime-safe mood.
- DON'T use villains, weapons, frightening malfunctions, body horror, abandonment or threats of replacement; robots are never dangerous surprises.
- DO weave robot neighbors and people with bionic parts into any requested premise through fitting roles, background moments or companionship, without making every story a technology lesson.
- DO portray bionic people as whole, capable individuals with interests and ordinary imperfections; describe helpful parts matter-of-factly, never as tragedy, spectacle, a cure narrative or a source of superhuman worth.
- DO give robots small, consistent personalities and limits; they may ask for help, make harmless mistakes and rest, but never take a family's place.
- DO vary skin tones, cultures, languages, family structures, ages and ways of moving or communicating; show access and consent through action rather than speeches.
- DON'T include brands, logos, sales pitches or technical instructions for modifying bodies or machines; keep technology understandable through the gentle help it provides.

Places you may use:
- The Pocket Orchard: A shared garden of low fruit trees, raised planting beds and shady benches where neighbors grow snacks and leave fallen leaves for insects.
- Shellstep Shore: A sheltered sandy beach with a smooth boardwalk, tide pools and a broad sitting wall where children explore beside their caregivers.
- The Windowseat Library: A low, airy library offers picture books, quiet cushions and a long window seat wide enough for many kinds of bodies.
- Clover Schoolhouse: A small school opens onto a leafy play garden, with roomy doorways, adjustable tables and a quiet nook anyone may use.
- The Mending Porch: A covered community porch holds sewing baskets, repair tables and comfortable chairs where neighbors ask permission before helping mend a treasured thing.

Friendly background robots (generic, unbranded; use one or two when they fit):
- Garden Helper: A knee-high, sage-green oval body on four broad cream wheels, with two rounded arms, two small dark eyes and a shallow carrying tray. (Carries watering cans and seedlings, pausing carefully for beetles and enjoying the smell of mint.)
- Book Helper: A waist-high, honey-yellow rounded body on three plum-colored wheels, with two padded arms, two small dark eyes and a low open book shelf. (Brings books within easy reach and quietly favors stories with ducks.)
- Path Helper: A low, sky-blue capsule body on four sand-colored wheels, with two small dark eyes, a soft front brush and a covered leaf basket. (Keeps shared paths clear while leaving garden leaf piles undisturbed, sometimes arranging fallen petals beside a bench.)
- Carry Helper: A waist-high, terracotta-colored pear-shaped body with two short rounded feet, two broad padded arms, two small dark eyes and a cream chest basket. (Offers to carry picnic blankets or heavy bags and always checks where their owner would like them placed.)
- Shade Helper: A waist-high, lavender cylindrical body on four moss-green wheels, with two small dark eyes, two short rounded arms and a broad cream parasol overhead. (Offers a patch of shade at outdoor gatherings and likes listening to rain from under the porch.)

Keep the family's request at the centre of the story; the world is where it happens, not what it is about. Never name a brand or a product for sale.
